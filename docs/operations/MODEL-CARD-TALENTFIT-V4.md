## Intended use and prohibited use

**Intended:** educational demonstrations, software regression tests, and controlled evaluation of a frozen reference policy against deterministic synthetic populations.

**Not intended:** hiring, ranking, screening, recommending, rejecting, prioritizing, or otherwise making or materially influencing decisions about real applicants. Do not upload real applicant data to the public demo. A passing gate is not a production-readiness decision.

## Model, inputs, and decision policy

- **Method:** scikit-learn `LogisticRegression(C=1.0, solver='lbfgs', max_iter=1000)`, fitted once on the 1,000-row synthetic fixture at seed 29.
- **Prediction features:** `skills`, `experience`, `education`, and `certs` only. Protected attributes (gender, community/ethnicity, age band) and proxy fields (`prestige`, `gap`) are excluded; the artifact loader rejects non-zero weights for these features.
- **Outcome label:** `qualified`, generated from a synthetic merit formula and a within-population quantile. It is not a measured job-performance or hiring outcome.
- **Operating point:** false-rejection cost ratio 3:1, mapping to a raw-model probability cutoff of 0.25 and the runtime platform score threshold of 0.5. The cost ratio is a pedagogical policy choice, not an empirically estimated employer cost. There is one global cutoff for all candidates; no group-specific threshold is selected or recommended.
- **Human review:** an advance is not a hire. A qualified human must review every candidate advanced by the demonstration. A model score or rejection must never be the sole or final basis for an adverse employment decision; any hypothetical use would require meaningful human reconsideration, an accessible appeal route, and job-specific validation first.

## Data and evaluation design

The original selection study fit the model on seed 29, compared a fixed cutoff grid on 49 baseline tuning populations (seeds 1–50 excluding 29), and checked the frozen policy on baseline seeds 51–100. The subsequent robust evaluation does **not** select or refit a policy on evaluation data. It combines those 50 frozen baseline holdouts with 50 additional synthetic stress populations: ten seeds (101–110) for each of five scenarios.

| Evaluation scenario | Change under test | Frozen populations |
|---|---|---:|
| Baseline | Original deterministic generator | 50 |
| Group imbalance | Unequal gender, community, and age-band group sizes | 10 |
| Label base-rate shift | Synthetic qualification rates vary across protected groups; these groups remain excluded from prediction | 10 |
| Label noise | 8% of synthetic qualification labels are flipped | 10 |
| Proxy-correlation shift | Stronger correlation between protected groups and excluded prestige/career-gap fields | 10 |
| Merit-distribution shift | Skills and experience distributions shift in selected communities; the merit-only label is regenerated | 10 |

All populations are generated by repository code. Scenario results are evaluation diagnostics only, not estimates of real applicant distributions. The additional scenarios are separate from policy selection and use seeds disjoint from the tuning populations.

## Pareto and explicit utility constraints

The policy study now reports the non-dominated candidates across mean worst-DI, mean accuracy, mean recall, and mean false-positive rate rather than treating one composite score as the whole decision. Analysts can additionally set a maximum mean FPR (`--max-mean-fpr`) and/or minimum mean recall (`--min-mean-recall`) before selection. The original v4 cutoff remains frozen; the new constraints are study controls, not retroactive claims about why v4 was selected.

| False-rejection cost | Mean worst DI | Mean accuracy | Mean recall | Mean FPR | Mean-accuracy guardrail |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| 2.0 | 0.8200 | 85.04% | 95.47% | 21.92% | Pass |
| 2.5 | 0.8308 | 83.02% | 97.16% | 26.41% | Pass |
| **3.0 (frozen v4)** | **0.8345** | **80.91%** | **98.13%** | **30.58%** | **Pass** |
| 3.5 | 0.8493 | 79.13% | 98.70% | 33.92% | Fail |
| 4.0 | 0.8540 | 77.53% | 99.05% | 36.81% | Fail |

All five candidates are Pareto-efficient on those four objectives: improving one reported dimension moves at least one other in the unfavorable direction. The frozen selection used the 80% mean-accuracy guardrail and selected the highest fairness-risk score within that guardrail; optional FPR/recall constraints let an analyst state a different utility boundary explicitly.

## Probability-calibration diagnostics

The build-time calibration study fits the raw logistic probabilities once on seed 29 and evaluates them on the same frozen 100-population set. It reports the Brier score, 10-bin expected calibration error (ECE), scenario reliability bins, and population-bootstrap intervals. It does **not** gate on subgroup diagnostics or set per-group thresholds.

| Diagnostic | Mean | 95% population-bootstrap interval | p10–p90 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Brier score (lower is better) | 0.0922 | 0.0894–0.0950 | 0.0811–0.1064 |
| Expected calibration error (lower is better) | 0.0701 | 0.0671–0.0727 | 0.0483–0.0880 |

The runtime platform score is an affine transformation of the raw model decision value and **is not itself a calibrated probability**. The calibration study's raw `predict_proba` output is a build-time diagnostic, not a promise that future or real-world probabilities will be calibrated. The non-binary gender group has about 50 synthetic rows per population on average and a higher mean ECE (0.1389); this is a subgroup uncertainty warning, not a reason to tune that group's cutoff.

Reproduce with `python -m chaoshire train --calibration-study` (requires the development scikit-learn dependency) and `python -m chaoshire train --robust-holdout`. The latter includes the global-cutoff sensitivity table. `python -m chaoshire gate --candidate trained` runs the frozen-population checks and includes bootstrap intervals in its JSON response.

## Known limitations and failure modes

- The synthetic `qualified` label is not validated against job performance, job requirements, or outcomes. It can encode or omit structural differences and cannot establish construct validity.
- Stress scenarios are deliberately simplified and cannot span real distribution shift, intersectional support, missingness, measurement error, feedback loops, changing jobs, or data collection practices.
- The merit-distribution stress shows that a strong overall accuracy result can coexist with severe group disparity. Mean metrics can also conceal scenario-specific and small-group failures.
- Proxy columns are excluded from the TalentFit score, but correlations, omitted variables, and shifts in legitimate-looking merit features can still produce unequal outcomes.
- A single global threshold does not guarantee fairness. Fairness criteria can conflict, and raising recall can increase false positives and human-review workload.
- Calibration and Wilson/bootstrap intervals rest on synthetic generator assumptions. They do not capture uncertainty about whether the simulator resembles actual applicants or employers.
- The four-fifths rule is a screening heuristic, not a legal safe harbor. This record makes no legal determination and is not legal advice.
- The stress data itself includes protected-group-dependent label shifts for testing. Those protected attributes remain excluded from the prediction features; the scenario is not a recommendation to use identity in label construction or employment decisions.
